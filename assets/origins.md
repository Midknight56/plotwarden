# Where the art comes from

Every drawing in this folder was drawn for this project, by hand, as SVG paths. None of it is traced from a photograph, a stock library, a logo or a font. There is no real grave, no real site plan and no real person in any of it.

| File | What it is | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| `gate-lantern.svg` | One gate lantern, cut out in ivory with a warm glass panel, hanging from an iron arm | The sky strip at the top of the Churchyard Register, in the right margin |
| `churchyard-margin.svg` | A low line of mist with a few dark yew trees. It tiles sideways without a seam | The foot of the sky strip, in the margin only. It drifts slowly. It stops for reduced motion and in high contrast |
| `garden-margin.svg` | A row of furrows with young plants, a small tool shed, a garden gate and a few clouds and a sun. It tiles sideways without a seam | The daylight band at the top of the Allotment Register, in the margin only. It drifts very slowly. It stops for reduced motion and in high contrast |
| `plot-symbols.svg` | The five marks of the map key as symbols: Free (open arch), Reserved (slash), Taken (solid centre), Unusable (cross), Needs checking (dotted arch) | The master drawing of the marks. The key and the state mark beside the selected plot draw the same arch and marks in code, so the register loads no extra file for them |

Colours. Churchyard: ink `#22252C` on ivory `#FAF5E9`, a dark indigo sky `#232A5C`, and one warm amber `#F2C96B` for the lantern glass. Garden: ink `#1B312A` on pale paper `#F7F5E8`, a daylight sky `#CFE7DF`, leaf green `#2F6B4F`, pale soil `#EFE6CC` and a sun yellow `#F2C94C`. Nothing in the art carries meaning by colour alone: each map mark has its word beside it.

The art holds no text and no script, and it never sits over a word or a control. Each stylesheet gives it its own band above the title.

The beds on the garden plan (a footprint, a strip of hatching and a square peg for the code) and the grass walks are drawn in code by the shared sheet and the visitor map. They use no file.

Lantern Fold and Thimble Beds are invented places. Their names, plots, inscriptions and payments were made for this project.
